KEMS UAV RESEARCH PROJECT

OBSTACLE AVOIDANCE-BASED AUTONOMOUS NAVIGATION OF A QUADROTOR SYSTEM

Modeling, simulating, and modifying quadrotor differential equations for precision livestock monitoring and autonomous obstacle avoidance.

PROJECT ORIGIN & CONTEXT ⏱️ 2 Min Read

The Shepherd’s New Wings: A Tale of Pastures, Mathematics & Autonomous Flight

Across the rolling savanna pastures of the Afram Plains, livestock farming is vulnerable to unpredictable terrain, dense acacia thorn bushes, and unmonitored blind spots where cattle and sheep scatter. Tracking herds manually over hundreds of hectares under harsh heat is exhausting and risky.

"How can an autonomous aerial robot navigate complex pastures, scout grazing livestock, and steer around obstacles on its own?"

The solution lies in the mathematics of six ordinary differential equations derived from Newton's Second Law and Euler angular dynamics. By continuously measuring altitude, position, and tilt angles, the quadrotor modulates its four motor speeds to maintain stable hover.

When an obstacle (like a farm tree or fence) is detected by the onboard 2D LiDAR rangefinder, the controller injects a tangential repulsive steering force into the equations of motion—naturally bending the trajectory around the obstacle with a safe 1.0-meter clearance threshold.

Understanding the Research Paper

Overview of the quadrotor autonomous navigation study (Alanezi et al., MDPI Drones 2022, 6, 288).

What is this paper about?

The research paper investigates how a 4-propeller drone (quadrotor) can autonomously fly across a farm, maintain a steady altitude of 5 meters, and steer around obstacles (like trees or sheep) without crashing.

To make the drone autonomous, the authors established a 3-tier navigation framework:

  • Perception: An onboard Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) tracks orientation and roll/pitch angles ($\phi, \theta$), while a 2D LiDAR rangefinder measures distances to obstacles in real time.
  • Planning: The Bat Algorithm path planner calculates safe detour steering angles whenever an obstacle enters the warning zone.
  • Control (Differential Equations): Newton-Euler equations of motion calculate physical accelerations, and dual Genetic Algorithm-tuned PID loops control altitude and attitude.

The 6 Differential Equations of Motion

Derived directly from Newton's Second Law ($F = m a$) and rotational dynamics ($T = I \alpha$).

1. Translational Position Equations ($\ddot{x}, \ddot{y}, \ddot{z}$)

These 3 equations tell us how the drone accelerates through 3D space. Remember from physics that acceleration is the second derivative of position ($\ddot{x} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$):

Forward & Sideways Acceleration (X and Y directions)
$$\ddot{x} = \frac{U_1}{m} \left( \cos\phi \sin\theta \cos\psi + \sin\phi \sin\psi \right)$$ $$\ddot{y} = \frac{U_1}{m} \left( \cos\phi \sin\theta \sin\psi - \sin\phi \cos\psi \right)$$
Vertical Altitude Acceleration (Z direction)
$$\ddot{z} = \frac{U_1}{m} \left( \cos\phi \cos\theta \right) - g$$
$U_1$ = Total upward lifting thrust produced by all 4 motors together ($U_1 = f_1 + f_2 + f_3 + f_4$).
$m$ = Total mass of the drone (1.888 kg).
$g$ = Gravity ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$). The drone only climbs when $\frac{U_1}{m}\cos\phi\cos\theta > g$.
$\phi, \theta, \psi$ = Roll, Pitch, and Yaw tilt angles. When the drone tilts forward (Pitch $\theta > 0$), part of $U_1$ pushes the drone in the X direction!

2. Rotational Angle Equations ($\ddot{\phi}, \ddot{\theta}, \ddot{\psi}$)

These 3 equations describe how the drone tilts and rotates when motor speeds are changed:

Roll, Pitch, and Yaw Angular Accelerations
$$\ddot{\phi} = \frac{U_2 \cdot l}{I_x} - \left( \frac{I_z - I_y}{I_x} \right) \dot{\theta} \dot{\psi} - \frac{J_r \omega_r}{I_x} \dot{\theta}$$ $$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{U_3 \cdot l}{I_y} - \left( \frac{I_x - I_z}{I_y} \right) \dot{\phi} \dot{\psi} + \frac{J_r \omega_r}{I_y} \dot{\phi}$$ $$\ddot{\psi} = \frac{U_4}{I_z} - \left( \frac{I_y - I_x}{I_z} \right) \dot{\phi} \dot{\theta}$$
$U_2$ = Roll control torque: difference in thrust between left and right motors ($f_4 - f_2$).
$U_3$ = Pitch control torque: difference in thrust between front and back motors ($f_3 - f_1$).
$U_4$ = Yaw control torque: difference in propeller drag spin.
$\dot{\theta} \dot{\psi}, \dot{\phi} \dot{\psi}$ = Gyroscopic cross-coupling terms (spinning bodies naturally interact when rotated).

Discussion of the Study Results

Critical evaluation of controller performance, obstacle margins, and research limitations.

1. Why ITAE Tuning is Better than ISE

The Genetic Algorithm optimized the PID gains using two different cost formulas:

$$\text{ISE} = \int_0^{t_{\text{sim}}} e^2(t)\,dt \qquad\qquad \text{ITAE} = \int_0^{t_{\text{sim}}} t \cdot |e(t)|\,dt$$

In Plain English:

  • ISE (Integral Square Error): Squares the error ($e^2$). Because big errors get squared into huge numbers, the controller kicks the motors violently at the start. It climbs fast ($0.73\text{ s}$) but overshoots by a huge 14.30%.
  • ITAE (Integral Time-weighted Absolute Error): Multiplies error by time ($t$). It doesn't panic at the start, but heavily penalizes any lingering error later. This produces smooth damping with only 2.65% overshoot.
  • Why it matters on a farm: Overshooting by 14% means a drone trying to hover at 5m will shoot up to 5.7m, which can crash into tree branches or drain the battery with sudden motor surges. ITAE is much safer!

2. Obstacle Threshold Analysis: 1.0 m vs. 0.5 m Margin

  • $1.0\text{ m}$ Threshold (Safe): Gives the drone enough room to tilt and steer around obstacles up to 1.0 meter wide (the size of an adult sheep) with zero collisions.
  • $0.5\text{ m}$ Threshold (Crash): The drone spans $0.45\text{ m}$ across its arms. Because it takes time to bank and turn, forward momentum causes the drone to hit the obstacle before it can steer away.

3. Limitations of the Original Paper

Four major gaps we identified that our project addresses:

1. No Wind Disturbance

The paper assumed perfectly calm air. Real farms have constant crosswinds and gusts.

2. No Air Resistance (Drag)

The equations omitted aerodynamic drag on the drone body during forward flight.

3. Static Obstacles Only

All obstacles were stationary. Real sheep move around dynamically.

4. Fixed Mass Assumption

No testing on how adding camera or sensor payloads changes flight stability.

Our Model Modifications & Interactive Simulator

How we added environmental crosswinds, aerodynamic air resistance, and variable mass to the differential equations.

Our Modified Differential Equations

We modified the paper's original position equations by adding wind disturbance force $F_w$ and air resistance drag $-k_d v$:

Modified X-Acceleration Equation (with Crosswind and Drag)
$$\ddot{x} = \underbrace{\frac{U_1}{m}\left(\cos\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi + \sin\phi\sin\psi\right)}_{\text{Motor Thrust Force}} - \underbrace{\frac{k_d}{m} v_x \|\mathbf{v}\|}_{\text{Air Resistance Drag}} + \underbrace{\frac{F_{w,x}(t)}{m}}_{\text{Crosswind Force}}$$
Modified Z-Acceleration Equation (Altitude with Payload Mass)
$$\ddot{z} = \frac{U_1}{m}\left(\cos\phi\cos\theta\right) - g - \frac{k_d}{m} v_z \|\mathbf{v}\|$$
$F_{w,x}(t)$ = Lateral wind force: steady crosswind plus gust waves $A_{\text{gust}}\sin(2\pi f t)$.
$k_d$ = Drag coefficient ($0.45\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$). Dampens high speeds realistically.
$m$ = Dynamic payload mass ($1.4\text{ kg}$ to $2.5\text{ kg}$). Adding a camera makes the drone heavier, requiring more thrust.

Our Results: Analytical Solution & ODE Solvers

Solving the equations analytically, comparing numerical solvers in MATLAB, and error convergence.

1. Analytical Closed-Form Solution (Linearized 2nd-Order Altitude ODE)

When hovering near horizontal level ($\phi \approx 0, \theta \approx 0$), the altitude differential equation simplifies to a classic second-order linear ODE with constant coefficients:

$$\ddot{z} + 2\zeta\omega_n\dot{z} + \omega_n^2 z = \omega_n^2 z_{\text{target}}$$ $$\text{Exact Closed-Form Solution: } z(t) = z_{\text{target}} \left( 1 - \frac{e^{-\zeta\omega_n t}}{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} \sin\left(\omega_d t + \phi_{\text{phase}}\right) \right)$$
Natural frequency ($\omega_n$) = $\sqrt{K_p} = \sqrt{5.00} = 2.236\text{ rad/s}$.
Damping ratio ($\zeta$) = $\frac{K_d}{2\sqrt{K_p}} = \frac{3.6485}{2\sqrt{5}} = 0.8158$ (Underdamped, very stable).
Damped frequency ($\omega_d$) = $\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2} = 1.293\text{ rad/s}$.

3. Step-Size Error Convergence: Forward Euler vs. Classical RK4

Log-log plot of step size $h$ vs. Global Truncation Error (RMSE). Forward Euler scales as $O(h)$ (slope = 1), while RK4 scales as $O(h^4)$ (slope = 4) — cutting step size in half drops RK4 error by 16 times!

4. Numerical Stability: Forward Euler Breakdown vs. RK4 Robustness

Forward Euler is only conditionally stable ($h < h_{\text{crit}} \approx \frac{2}{\omega_n} \approx 0.8\text{s}$, and for coupled 2nd-order systems $h < 0.1\text{s}$). When $h = 0.25\text{s}$, Euler diverges and blows up, while Classical RK4 remains completely stable.

Further Research Frontiers

7 future research avenues for smart agricultural drones.

FRONTIER 1

Multi-Drone Swarms

Extending equations from 1 drone to a team of 5 drones that share radar data and monitor hundreds of sheep simultaneously without colliding.

FRONTIER 2

GPS-Denied Tree Canopy Navigation

Using downward optical-flow cameras so the drone can navigate under dense forest canopy where satellite GPS signals are blocked.

Alternative Tuning Algorithms

3D Dynamic Avoidance

Hardware Microcontroller Testing

Applied Programming Portfolio & Codebase

1. Software Architecture & 3-Tier Navigation Pipeline

Our software implementation follows the 3-tier autonomous navigation pipeline established in the paper (Perception $\rightarrow$ Planning $\rightarrow$ Control), translating the mathematical differential equations into modular MATLAB R2024b functions:

1. Perception Module

Reads simulated IMU sensor data (roll $\phi$, pitch $\theta$, yaw $\psi$) and 2D LiDAR rangefinder distances to livestock and trees.

2. Planning & Evasion

Implements the Bat Algorithm obstacle evasion vector, generating desired banking angles ($\phi_d, \theta_d$) when distance $d < 1.0\text{m}$.

3. Control & Integration

Solves the 6 coupled ODEs forward in time using ode45, rk4_solver, and euler_solver to update position $(x, y, z)$.
